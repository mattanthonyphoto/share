Traffic

The numbers.
Up 31% year over year.

Squarespace Analytics, May 1 to July 28, 2026. The direction of travel is good — the growth is real and it is broad.

The Quarter
✓
912 visits — up 31% year over year
Monthly: May 274, June 326, July 312 through the 28th. July works out to about 11.1 visits a day, putting it on pace to finish ahead of June. The trend is up across the whole quarter.
✓
740 unique visitors — up 26%
More people, not simply more visits from the same people.
✓
2,400 pageviews — up 39%
Pageviews growing faster than visits means people are going deeper into the site rather than landing and leaving. That is roughly 2.6 pages per visit, which is healthy for a portfolio site.
!
Bounce rate 58.43% — up 18%, and this one is a negative
Squarespace shows this with an up arrow, but for bounce rate up is bad: a larger share of visitors leave without viewing a second page. At 58% it is still within normal range for a design portfolio, but the direction matters. The 15-second mobile load time is a prime suspect.

What We Still Cannot See

These numbers tell us how many people arrive. They do not tell us which searches bring them — the single most useful input for deciding what to write next.

Two more screenshots from Squarespace (the Search Keywords and Traffic Sources tabs) would close that gap immediately. Google Search Console access would close it permanently.

Kadenwood — Whistler
The Critical Finding

The commercial pages
are switched off.

I crawled all 59 URLs twice today — once this afternoon, and again this evening after the site was changed. Sixteen finished landing pages went offline between the two crawls.

What I Found
✗
16 finished landing pages now return a 404
Fully written pages — real copy, project case studies, FAQ blocks, calls to action — covering every location (Whistler, Squamish, Pemberton, Vancouver), every kitchen page, the awards page and the interior design cost guide. They were sitting on Squarespace placeholder addresses and needed renaming. They were switched off instead.
✗
11,463 words are no longer visible to Google
The site went from roughly 27,500 words of indexable content down to 13,632 — about half. What went with it was specifically the commercial layer: the pages built to win searches like "Whistler interior design" and "kitchen design Squamish".
✗
A page that was ranking went offline with them
/whistler-interior-designers had 7 internal links pointing at it — the only page in this group properly wired into the site — and it was appearing in live search results for Squamish and Whistler interior design queries. Google will drop it from the index on its next crawl.
!
The retired pages have no redirects
Two older Whistler pages were reasonable to retire, but they should have been forwarded to their replacements rather than left as dead ends. As it stands their accumulated ranking value is discarded rather than passed on.
!
The sitemap still lists all 59 URLs
24 of them now return a 404. Squarespace will catch up on its own, but until it does the site is actively telling Google to crawl two dozen dead pages.

This Is Recoverable

Squarespace keeps the content of a disabled page. Re-enabling each one and applying the correct address brings all 11,463 words back — and delivers the fix these pages needed in the first place.

Order matters: rename first, then re-enable. Done that way the site keeps the clean link structure it has now and gets its commercial content back. Two to three hours.

Panorama — Whistler
Technical Foundation

The fundamentals.
Genuinely strong.

Separate from the content question, the technical health of this site is good — and this week's cleanup improved it.

What's Working
✓
Structured data on every page
LocalBusiness, InteriorDesigner, WebSite, FAQ blocks, and blog posts correctly attributed to Lauren Ritz. This is ahead of most studios in the region.
✓
Perfect Lighthouse SEO score
100/100 on both mobile and desktop.
✓
The broken-link problem is resolved
Before the changes there were 264 broken internal links across 24 pages. There is now exactly one left on the entire site.
✓
Clean titles, addresses and security
Valid SSL, lrdstudio.ca forwards correctly to www, http forwards to https, canonical tags on every live page, and no duplicate title tags remain.
✓
AI search engines have full access
ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google's AI crawlers are all permitted. Many Squarespace sites block these by default. This matters as more clients begin their search inside an AI assistant.
✓
Alt text is essentially complete
Only 28 images missing alt text sitewide, and 27 of those sit on one internal working page. The rest of the site is clean.
What Needs Fixing
✗
A duplicate homepage is still indexed
/home is a full copy of the homepage with no canonical tag telling Google which version is authoritative. Google currently has it indexed separately.
✗
Two internal working pages are still public
/clientname is a blank client page template, and /project-banner-images is an image working file holding 27 images with no alt text. Neither has an H1 or a meta description, and both are reachable by anyone.
!
21 text contrast failures
One muted grey (#7b7164 on #f8f6f2) sits just under the accessibility standard at 4.35 against a 4.5 threshold. Darkening that single colour clears all 21 at once.

Technical Bottom Line

The foundation scores 88/100, and nothing on the fix list is structural. Every item is a settings change or a short edit.

Balsam — Whistler
Content & Speed

Good writing.
Slow on a phone.

The written content is strong where it exists. The gaps are the project pages and the mobile experience.

Content
✓
Project page descriptions are well written
16 of the 17 project pages have their own distinct, well-crafted meta description. Only one genuine duplicate exists — Falcon and Toni Sailer share the same text, so one of the two needs rewriting.
!
Project pages average 269 words
Beautiful photography, very little story. Each one wants 400 or more words on the brief, the materials, the constraints and the collaborators. This is exactly what the Content Multiplier work produces from each shoot.
!
Blog posts show no date
Three posts are live and the publication dates exist in the page code, but they are not displayed. Visible dates help both search freshness and reader confidence.
!
No Squamish or Pemberton project pages
Both are target locations with no local project to point at. Worth prioritising in an upcoming quarterly shoot.
Speed
✗
Mobile load time is 15.4 seconds
Measured on a typical mobile connection. Google ranks on the mobile experience, most enquiries arrive on a phone, and this is the most likely explanation for the rising bounce rate. Desktop is fine at 2.8 seconds.
✗
3.2 seconds of that is Squarespace's own stylesheet
A single 110KB platform file blocks the page from drawing anything at all. The custom navigation logo is the element Google times, and it cannot appear until that file finishes loading.
!
862KB of oversized images on the homepage
Hero images are delivered far larger than they display — 369KB, 285KB, 265KB and 219KB. These can be resized at delivery without touching the originals.
✓
Nothing shifts, and the server is fast
Zero layout shift as the page loads, and the server responds in under 120ms. The delay is entirely in what loads afterwards.

On Mobile Speed

A large share of the 3.8MB page weight is Squarespace platform overhead that cannot be removed on this plan. Preloading the logo and resizing the heroes will help immediately.

The full fix is the custom-coded rebuild already in the Creative Partner scope.

What I'd Fix First

The action plan.
In priority order.

Ordered by impact per hour of work. The first item is worth more than everything below it combined.

1

Restore the 16 pages, renaming as you go

For each one: apply the correct address from the table above first, write a real title and meta description, then switch it back on. Done in that order the site keeps the clean link structure it has now and gets 11,463 words of finished content back on keyword-relevant addresses. Two to three hours.

2

Bring back the page that was ranking

/whistler-interior-designers was appearing in live search results and had 7 internal links pointing at it. Restore it, or forward it to /whistler-interior-design so its ranking value carries across. Do not leave it dead.

3

Add redirects for anything retired

Every page taken down should forward to its replacement rather than return an error. That passes on whatever ranking value it built up instead of discarding it.

4

Resubmit the sitemap

Once the addresses are correct, resubmit in Search Console so Google stops crawling two dozen dead URLs and finds the restored pages quickly.

5

Link the restored pages into the site

Add the location and service pages to the main navigation or a services hub. Previously they only linked to each other, which was not enough on its own.

6

Clean up what is left

Resolve the duplicate /home, and take down or hide /clientname and /project-banner-images. Darken one muted grey to clear all 21 contrast failures at once.

7

Turn on proper search reporting

Google Analytics is installed and collecting, but we have no reporting access and no Search Console access. Granting both means we can see the actual searches bringing people in, not just how many arrive.

The Short Version

Traffic is up 31% year on year and the technical foundation is genuinely strong. The expensive work — the research, the writing, the photography, the structured data — is already done.

What the site needs is its commercial pages switched back on, with the right addresses this time. That one afternoon roughly doubles the content Google can see.
